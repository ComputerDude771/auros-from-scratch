AurOS 0.1.0 · Nocturne · pre-release

Your ten‑year‑old PC still has a decade left in it.

AurOS is a complete Linux desktop that installs from inside Windows. You download one file and answer plain-language questions. Your Windows partition is not erased: it stays on the disk, stays bootable, and stays the default until you decide otherwise.

Download for Windows See exactly what happens

Pre-release, and we will keep saying so until it isn’t. The only thing AurOS offers today is AurBridge’s compatibility check — a read-only program that inspects your PC and tells you whether AurOS could install safely on it. It never writes to your disk, and there is no public build of it published yet. The installer itself ships when the path that puts Windows back is finished and tested, not before.

The promise, stated exactly

Three claims. Each one has a limit, and the limit is printed next to it.

  1. 01

    Your files come with you

    On first boot, AurOS reads your Windows partition and brings over your documents, pictures, music, downloads, desktop, Wi-Fi passwords, wallpaper, language and keyboard layout.

    The limit Chrome and Edge passwords need your old Windows password. OneDrive files that live only in the cloud are not real files on the disk and cannot be copied. You get a written report of everything we could not bring. What can’t move.

  2. 02

    Windows stays on the disk

    We shrink the Windows partition to make room; we never delete it. After the install, your PC still shows a boot menu and Windows is still the default until you change it yourself.

    The limit Shrinking a partition is the one genuinely destructive step, and it is where a failing disk or a power cut does real damage. That is why we check the drive first and refuse if it looks sick. What can go wrong.

  3. 03

    We refuse PCs we can’t handle

    Before anything is touched, AurBridge reads your firmware mode, disk health, encryption state, partition layout and power source. If any of it is unsafe, it stops and tells you why, in words, with what to do about it.

    The limit A refusal is a real outcome, not a bug. Some PCs — legacy BIOS machines, dynamic disks, drives with bad sectors — will simply never be accepted, with no override checkbox anywhere.

What AurOS actually is

A desktop operating system built around one idea: the person using it should never have to know what a partition is.

Written here

The desktop shell, the theme engine, the init system, the package manager, the installer and the migration engine are ours, written from scratch. That means no upstream theme fighting our theme and no policies we inherited from someone else’s project. Everything you look at, and everything an administrator configures, is a surface we control end to end.

Borrowed on purpose

The kernel, the drivers, the firmware blobs and the graphics stack come from a long-term-support Linux base. Twenty years of quirk handling — Wi-Fi chipsets, backlight paths, suspend on a 2013 laptop — is not something anyone reproduces, and on an old PC that quirk handling is the product working or not working.

Nobody writes their own graphics driver. Claiming otherwise would be the kind of make-believe this project was built to avoid.

Why two restarts and not one

The original design did everything from inside live Windows and rebooted once. We dropped it. Between the first destructive write and a working Linux boot there would be no environment in which recovery code could run — fail at 60% and the machine boots into nothing. The second restart buys a rescue environment that tests your Wi-Fi, screen brightness and sound on the real machine before anything is committed. How that works.

What it looks like

Nocturne is the default theme: deep atmospheric blue-black with an aurora-teal glow. The wallpaper is not a photograph — it is drawn by the system at your screen’s exact resolution from a handful of numbers, so it is sharp on any panel and weighs nothing.

Who this is for

A good fit if…

  • You have a Windows 10 or 11 PC that has become slow, and buying a new one is not the plan.
  • Your PC still runs, boots reliably and has around 28 GB free.
  • What you do is mostly a web browser, documents, photos, music and video calls.
  • You have somewhere to put a backup, and a spare USB stick.
  • You would rather keep Windows in the boot menu for the one thing that still needs it.

Not a good fit if…

  • This is your only computer and you have no backup and no USB stick. We will gate you out at the front door, deliberately.
  • You depend on a Windows-only application — a specific tax package, a CAD tool, an instrument driver. Those do not come across.
  • Your PC boots in legacy BIOS mode rather than UEFI. We refuse those; there is no safe way to undo a bad boot record on them.
  • Your drive is reporting bad sectors. Then the honest answer is: back up today, on any machine, and do not install anything.
  • You want a cloud sync of your files as part of the move. We do not upload your data anywhere. It never leaves the machine.

Start with the check, not the install

The compatibility check is read-only. It looks at your firmware, your disk, your encryption state and your power source, and it prints a plain list of what passed, what we would warn you about, and what would stop us. Running it changes nothing at all.
